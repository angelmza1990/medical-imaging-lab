Medical Imaging Lab

Method

What the simulations compute, how each piece is checked, and what they leave out.

The phantom

A synthetic axial slice of a head built from ellipses, in the spirit of the Shepp-Logan phantom: scalp fat, skull, a cortical ribbon of grey matter, white matter, deep grey matter, ventricles and two small lesions. It is rasterized at 256 × 256 with 3 × 3 supersampling, so pixels on a boundary hold a mix of tissues (partial volume), as real voxels do.

Relaxation times are representative values from published measurements (Stanisz et al. 2005; Wansapura et al. 1999; Lu et al. 2005), rounded. Measured values vary between studies, field strengths, subjects and methods by 10–20%. Attenuation is given in Hounsfield units typical of a head CT.

MRI

  • Bloch simulation: hard pulses are instantaneous rotations; between them each isochromat precesses at its own off-resonance frequency and relaxes exactly with T1 and T2. Off-resonance frequencies are quantiles of a Lorentzian, which makes the free induction decay exponential with 1/T2* = 1/T2 + 2π × half-width.
  • Contrast: closed-form steady-state signal equations for spin echo, inversion recovery and spoiled gradient echo (with T2* taken as 0.7 × T2). Noise is complex Gaussian, so magnitude images have Rician noise as on a scanner.
  • k-space: the 2D FFT of the image (radix-2, centred). The image is real-valued; real MR data carry a smooth phase from the coils and the field, which this simulation leaves out.
  • Compressed sensing: minimizes ‖MFx − y‖² + λ‖Wx‖₁ with FISTA and adaptive restart, using an orthonormal four-level Haar transform averaged over eight fixed shifts (translation-invariant thresholding). Sampling is by phase-encoding line, denser near the centre. Single coil, real-valued image.

CT

  • Projection: parallel-beam Radon transform with 256 detector bins, sampling each ray at half-pixel steps with bilinear interpolation.
  • Reconstruction: filtered back-projection. The ramp filter is the band-limited Ram-Lak kernel built in the spatial domain and transformed, optionally apodized by a Shepp-Logan or Hann window; sampling the ramp directly in frequency would shift every value by tens of HU.
  • Noise: detected photons follow a Poisson distribution (approximated as Gaussian) around I₀ e−∫μ, with water at 0.2 cm⁻¹. The geometry is far coarser than a clinical scanner's (about 1,000 views and 900 detectors), so the photon counts that give clinical noise levels here are not the clinical ones.

Tests

The numerical core is tested against analytic results, run with node --test tests/*.test.js on every change:

  • the FFT against a direct DFT, Parseval's theorem and exact inversion;
  • signal equations at their limits, the inversion-recovery null at TI = T1 ln 2 and the Ernst angle;
  • Bloch relaxation against the exponentials, a spin echo refocusing to e−TE/T2, and the T2* decay of a Lorentzian spread;
  • the Radon transform of a disk against 2√(R² − r²), and filtered back-projection recovering grey matter, white matter and fluid within 3 HU;
  • the Haar transform's orthonormality, and compressed sensing beating zero filling.

Limits

These are teaching simulations on a 2D synthetic phantom: no multiple coils, no B₀ or B₁ inhomogeneity maps, no chemical shift, diffusion, flow or magnetization transfer in MRI, and no beam hardening, scatter or fan-beam geometry in CT. They are not a medical device and their images are not real patient data.
