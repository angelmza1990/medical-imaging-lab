Medical Imaging Lab

Scanners that see inside the body without a single cut are, underneath, machines for solving inverse problems. These simulations follow an image from the physics that creates the signal to the algorithms that turn it into a picture, all running in your browser on a synthetic head.

Where the field is going

Faster scans. MRI is slow because k-space is measured line by line. Parallel imaging with many receiver coils, compressed sensing and, increasingly, neural networks trained on raw scanner data now cut scan times several-fold. Open datasets of raw k-space, led by fastMRI, made this a field anyone can work in.

Lower dose. In CT the limit is radiation. Iterative and deep-learning reconstructions recover diagnostic images from fewer photons, and photon-counting detectors measure the energy of each X-ray, separating materials such as iodine and calcium.

Quantitative imaging. Instead of images in arbitrary units, techniques such as MR fingerprinting measure T1, T2 and other tissue properties directly, so values can be compared across scanners and over time.

Learning-based analysis. Segmentation networks (U-Net and its self-configuring successor nnU-Net), foundation models trained on millions of images and tools for registration and detection are moving from research into clinical software, with validation, bias and regulation as the open questions.

Paths to take it further

Physics and simulation

Model the scanner itself and generate synthetic data.

  • MRI: KomaMRI.jl, JEMRIS, MRiLab
  • CT: ASTRA Toolbox, TIGRE, ODL
  • Start: reproduce a spin echo, then a full 2D sequence

Reconstruction

The most mathematical path: Fourier analysis, inverse problems, optimization.

  • SigPy and BART for MRI reconstruction
  • fastMRI raw k-space data (knee, brain)
  • Start: SENSE with multi-coil data, then a learned reconstruction

Image analysis and AI

Work on reconstructed images, mostly in Python.

  • pydicom, nibabel, SimpleITK, scikit-image
  • MONAI, TorchIO, nnU-Net for deep learning
  • Data: TCIA, BraTS, LIDC-IDRI, OpenNeuro

Clinical tools and standards

How images are stored, viewed and shared in hospitals.

  • DICOM and NIfTI formats, PACS
  • 3D Slicer, ITK-SNAP, OHIF viewer
  • Start: open a DICOM series, export it to NIfTI, measure a volume
